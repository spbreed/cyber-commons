01 / Why now

An intruder is through in less time than a meeting.

Breakout time — first access to moving laterally — has collapsed to 29 minutes. The fastest on record is 27 seconds. Nothing reviewed on a quarterly cycle keeps pace with that.

02 / The gap

The defence is real. It is just not evenly distributed.

Map the controls onto the industries that carry them and the same shape appears every time: capability thins as organisations get smaller, and thins again towards the newest controls.

  • Recovery is where it breaksIn healthcare, detection reaches 60% and recovery 22%. Those organisations can tell you an attack is happening and not tell you when the clinical system comes back.
  • Two-thirds of districts have nobody66% of school districts have no full-time cybersecurity position at all, and 12% allocate no cyber budget. The columns to the right of that are not close.
  • The newest column is empty for everyoneAgent security is where even the best-funded row is partial. For every row beneath it, no sector-level figure has been published at all.

04 / Who curates it

Curated by practitioners who ship this under audit.

The content is written, and regularly re-reviewed, by senior practitioners working in regulated industries — where a control has to satisfy an auditor as well as an attacker. Holding it to that standard is what makes it worth giving away.

  • Audit-grade by defaultEvery lesson names a risk, a control and a lab — the shape a regulator asks for, which happens to be the shape that teaches well.
  • Evidence, not screenshotsLabs produce output that is committed and re-run, so a claimed result has actually been produced.
  • Given away on purposeLevelling the field is the point. Hoarded security knowledge makes the whole network less safe.
05 / How it works

Open it, run it, change it, run it again.

A lesson opens as a notebook in your own Kaggle account, runs a real agent skill whose source you can read, and maps what it did back to the open frameworks your organisation already reports against. No install, no GPU, no licence.

  • Runs where you areA browser and a Kaggle account. The same lesson runs offline against a deterministic stand-in, or against an open-weight model you serve yourself.
  • Skills, not slideware138 agent skills, and 131 lessons execute one. You can open the source of every one of them.
  • Built on open sourceCNCF and Linux Foundation projects for the control plane, open-weight models for the intelligence. Frontier models optional, never required.
