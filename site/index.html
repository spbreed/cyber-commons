01 / Why now

An intruder is through in less time than a meeting.

Breakout time — how long it takes an attacker to get from first access to moving sideways — is now 29 minutes. The fastest on record is 27 seconds. Now look at how most security work is done: architecture reviewed every few months, alerts read one at a time, controls checked once a year. Attackers are already using AI to go faster. You cannot close that gap by reading faster — you have to change how the work gets done.

02 / The gap

Knowing the risk is not the same as being able to close it.

Most teams can name the risk. Far fewer have had the chance to learn how to do the work differently, and capability thins as organisations get smaller — then thins again for the newest controls. Buying a product does not change how a team works. Learning to do the work a new way does, and that is what each track teaches.

  • Recovery is where it breaksIn healthcare, detection reaches 60% and recovery 22%. Those organisations can tell you an attack is happening and not tell you when the clinical system comes back.
  • Two-thirds of districts have nobody66% of school districts have no full-time cybersecurity position at all, and 12% allocate no cyber budget. The columns to the right of that are not close.
  • The newest column is empty for everyoneAgent security is where even the best-funded row is partial. For every row beneath it, no sector-level figure has been published at all.
03 / The mission, and who writes it

Not only how to use AI. How to justify it, build it, and keep it running.

Teaching the technique is the easy half. Changing how a team works means answering three questions, in order, and every lesson here answers all three — Day 0, why this is worth doing at all; Day 1, the thing you build; and Day 2, the number that tells you next quarter that it still works.

Day 0 · why

What goes wrong if you do nothing, and why this is worth an afternoon.

Day 1 · how

The concrete thing you stand up — a control, a pipeline stage, a detection.

Day 2 · measure

The number that says it worked, and keeps saying so afterwards.

Getting that work funded is the real challenge, and it is hardest in budget-constrained organisations — the ones that cannot buy the capability and are not going to be sold it either. A curriculum that stops at the technique leaves you with nothing to take to the person holding the budget. This one has to carry all three days, on every lesson.

It is written by practitioners who ship this under audit — senior people working in regulated industries, where a control has to satisfy an auditor as well as an attacker. The material and the building blocks are updated continuously, so they keep pace with what those practitioners are actually seeing.

  • Day 0 is what gets given away on purposeThe technique is teachable anywhere. The argument for spending the quarter on it is what most material leaves out, and it is exactly what a constrained team cannot buy — so it is the part given away first.
  • Audit-grade by defaultEvery lesson names a risk, a control and a lab — the shape a regulator asks for, which happens to be the shape that teaches well.
  • Reviewed on every releaseA lesson written once describes a threat model that has already moved, so these are revisited against OWASP LLM & Agentic Top 10, MITRE ATLAS and NIST AI RMF as those change.

05 / How you learn it

Hands on from the first minute. You finish with something working, not a certificate.

You do not learn a control by reading about it. Every lesson runs a real agent skill whose source you can read — as a script, or inside the agent CLI you already use — and maps what it did back to the open frameworks your organisation already reports against. Then you change something and run it again, and watch the number move — which is the part that builds the instinct. You do not leave with notes. You leave with a control working, a pipeline stage running or a detection written, and the number that tells you later whether it is still doing its job. No install, no GPU, no licence.

  • Evidence, not screenshotsEvery skill is executed in CI with no model reachable, and has to refuse legibly rather than answer anyway. A result on this site was produced by a named model, not described.
  • Skills, not slideware140 agent skills, and 147 lessons execute one. You can open the source of every one of them, and edit it.
  • Built on open sourceCNCF and Linux Foundation projects for the control plane, open-weight models for the intelligence. Frontier models optional, never required.
