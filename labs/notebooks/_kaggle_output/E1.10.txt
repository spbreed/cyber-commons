22 controls across 5 functions
1. agent traces are full of personal data
   cyber           assumed: privacy owns retention of anything containing personal data
   privacy         assumed: security owns the log store, so security sets its schedule
   -> no schedule was set; three years of prompts are discoverable

2. the model was validated, the tools were not
   model_risk      assumed: validation covered the model, which is our scope
   cyber           assumed: MRM signed it off, so the deployment was approved
   -> an agent holds production write access that was never in scope

3. 'no training on our data' was negotiated, never instrumented
   legal           assumed: the clause is in the contract and it is binding
   cyber           assumed: legal handled the vendor, so the restriction is handled
   -> nobody built the control that verifies the vendor honours it

4. the use case was risk-tiered before it had tools
   compliance      assumed: classified low-risk: it was a chatbot when we saw it
   business_owner  assumed: we shipped features, not a new use case
   -> it files tickets, sends mail and moves money at the low-risk tier

Neither assumption in any pair is unreasonable. That is what makes these
seams rather than mistakes - and why naming the handoff is the control.
