There is deliberately no third column ranking these. Seconds of exposure
and wrongly-contained agents are not in the same unit, and any single
score that ranks them has smuggled in an exchange rate somebody chose.
Choose it openly, per incident class, or do not choose it at all.

The automated tier contains in a quarter of a minute and is wrong eight
times in a hundred, with nobody between the mistake and the estate. The
manual tier is wrong far less often and takes over half an hour, which on
a 29-minute breakout time means containment lands after the attacker has
finished. Neither is 'safe'. They fail differently.

This is why the tier comes from the remediation policy and not from the
runbook author's confidence: the trade is a property of the ACTION, and
the false-positive rate is a property of the DETECTION. Neither of them
is a property of how bold the person writing the runbook was feeling.
